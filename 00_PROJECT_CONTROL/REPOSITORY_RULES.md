# W.I.S.D.O.M. Repository Rules

## 1. Canon is explicit

Material becomes project canon only through an explicit Operator decision or because it records an already-established project decision.

Research, brainstorming, hypotheses, examples, and proposed curriculum are noncanonical unless promoted explicitly.

## 2. Evidence and interpretation remain distinguishable

Research records should distinguish:

- source or observation;
- factual finding;
- interpretation;
- hypothesis;
- recommendation;
- unresolved question.

## 3. Theory and practice remain distinguishable

W.I.S.D.O.M. may teach both conceptual understanding and practical execution.

They should connect to one another without being collapsed into the same instructional layer.

## 4. Learner respect is mandatory

The project must not rely on pandering, condescension, artificial simplification, or elitism.

Beginners are treated as capable people learning unfamiliar systems.

## 5. Evidence before commitment

Market, curriculum, and product decisions should be informed by investigated facts where those facts can reasonably be obtained.

Untested assumptions remain hypotheses.

## 6. Project history is evidence, not automatic curriculum

S.W.A.R.M., G.A.M.E., and other project histories may supply useful educational evidence.

Their contents do not automatically become W.I.S.D.O.M. doctrine or course material.

## 7. Preserve provenance

When material enters from another project, research thread, experiment, or external source, its origin should remain identifiable.

## 8. Local Git history is the durable working record

A local commit records repository history.

It does not imply remote publication, deployment, canonical promotion outside W.I.S.D.O.M., or authority over another project.
