### Consolidated proposed starting rule

Investigate a candidate when the agent can identify a **specific task observation, problem, or explicit knowledge gap**, explain its relevance, and state an answerable question whose answer would change an engineering or research decision. Record the expected benefit, plausible adverse consequence, supporting and conflicting evidence or their absence, uncertainty, and a next inquiry that fits the current task grant.

A concrete task gap grounds the inquiry even when evidence for the remedy is absent. Generic persuasion, repetition, or an attractive projected percentage does not count as supporting observation. This remains a proposed interpretation of Boss's agreed starting line; no transition is performed in this investigation.

If the next inquiry would exceed the task, preserve the scoped observation and dependency rather than silently launch broader work. If the assigned task is expressly to study applicability, that applicability question may itself be in scope without granting authority over the target project.

### Minimal proposed author-facing card

| Field | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| Idea | Claim or proposed principle, preserving the original wording/reference. |
| Task anchor | Specific observation, problem, or acknowledged gap; task/source link and why it matters. |
| Inquiry | Answerable question, why its answer would matter, and next authorized way to investigate. |
| Expected effects | Plausible benefit and material adverse consequence; “none identified” must not imply none exists. |
| Evidence and uncertainty | Supporting/contradictory source references, absent evidence, unknowns; observation distinct from interpretation. |
| Scope and limits | This task's applicability, relevant grant/budget reference, and boundary of further work. |
| Relations | Related candidates and shared source events when present. |

The recorder supplies record ID, candidate/revision reference, task/reporter identity, timestamp, original-source reference, schema version, and known relevant configuration references. Unknown values stay explicit. Event identity is separate from the underlying source-event identity, so several reports can refer to one incident. No globally unique timestamp requirement.

Keep `maturity`, `proposed_disposition`, and `triage_outcome` separate. The proposed offline evaluation must not change live maturity or authorization. A later activated curation mechanism would need its exact authorized behavior settled; a research record never becomes runtime policy by being saved.
