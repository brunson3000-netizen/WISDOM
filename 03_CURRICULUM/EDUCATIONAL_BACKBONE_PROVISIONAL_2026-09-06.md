# Educational Backbone — Provisional

**Date:** 2026-09-06
**Status:** PROVISIONAL
**Area:** curriculum architecture / learning theory
**Authority:** noncanonical unless later elevated
**Source:** W.I.S.D.O.M. learning-theory research and subsequent Operator acceptance

## Emerging backbone

W.I.S.D.O.M. should investigate a course architecture based on **scaffolded cognitive apprenticeship through authentic AI work**.

The learner should:

1. encounter meaningful practical work;
2. be shown enough of the normally invisible reasoning to understand what expert behavior is doing;
3. act rather than only watch;
4. receive scaffolding appropriate to current competence;
5. have support reduced as competence becomes visible;
6. connect new concepts to earlier ones;
7. receive immediate, low-pressure feedback;
8. demonstrate understanding in ways that AI cannot simply perform on the learner's behalf;
9. revisit principles in changed contexts so transfer can be observed.

## Provisional core

The following survived the first research/falsification pass strongly enough for Provisional preservation:

- cognitive apprenticeship;
- scaffolding with deliberate fading;
- guided rather than unguided discovery;
- active learning;
- embedded metacognition and self-regulation;
- formative evidence and immediate feedback;
- AI-use guardrails that distinguish artifact completion from learner competence;
- deliberate transfer across changed contexts;
- retrieval and spacing as supporting mechanisms.

## Provisional, bounded

Project/problem-based learning remains useful only when sufficiently guided and when the project actually serves the concept being learned.

"Build something and learning will happen automatically" is rejected.

## Theory — hold

Productive failure remains interesting, especially for debugging and AI experimentation, but is not yet a governing instructional rule.

## Rejected directions

The following should not be treated as W.I.S.D.O.M. instructional defaults:

- front-load all theory before meaningful action;
- sink-or-swim / unguided discovery for novices;
- assume adult learners require little scaffolding;
- treat frustration as evidence of rigor;
- equate a successful AI-generated artifact with student understanding;
- use complexity itself as a status signal or quality metric;
- assume more agents, more tooling, or more governance automatically improves learning.

## Exam-free direction

The emerging direction is **exam-free by default, not evidence-free**.

Evidence of learning should be distributed through contextual retrieval, prediction, explanation, application, connection, and immediate feedback rather than concentrated into a high-pressure end-of-module exam.

The separate idea of offering an optional exam remains Provisional and is tracked independently because it can recreate status pressure even when formally optional.
