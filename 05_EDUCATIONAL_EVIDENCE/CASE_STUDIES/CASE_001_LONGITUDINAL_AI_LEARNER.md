# Case 001 — Longitudinal AI Learner

**Status:** ACTIVE OBSERVATIONAL CASE  
**Started:** approximately late August 2026  
**Evidence type:** mixed retrospective baseline followed by prospective observation

## Baseline

The learner reports beginning serious study and use of contemporary AI-assisted engineering approximately two weeks before 2026-09-06.

Background:

- not an AI/software-industry professional;
- basic prior programming familiarity;
- historical experience with Pascal approximately 35 years earlier;
- no recent professional software-engineering background;
- learning contemporary AI-assisted project work primarily through actual project construction.

This is therefore not a blank-slate learner.

The case may be particularly useful for studying a technically curious adult returning to software concepts through modern AI tools.

## Current learning environment

The learner is not following a predefined W.I.S.D.O.M. curriculum.

Instead, W.I.S.D.O.M. is observing the natural learning sequence produced while the learner attempts real goals.

This distinction is important because imposing the future course structure now would contaminate the observation of what difficulties emerge naturally.

## Research value

The case can help identify:

- prerequisite chains;
- unexpected conceptual dependencies;
- theory/practice bridges;
- recurring failure modes;
- useful AI interaction habits;
- project-architecture lessons;
- governance lessons;
- tool friction;
- places where excessive complexity appears;
- places where greater structure would have prevented wasted effort.

## Limitations

This learner:

- has some historical programming exposure;
- is highly motivated by active projects;
- is working on unusually sophisticated projects relatively early;
- has access to multiple capable AI systems.

Findings must therefore not automatically be generalized to every beginner.

## Current rule

Observe first.

Do not reshape the learner's ongoing work merely to manufacture a cleaner educational story.

The eventual course should be derived from evidence rather than forcing the evidence to resemble the eventual course.
